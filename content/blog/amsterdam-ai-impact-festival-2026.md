On 24 November the [AI Impact Festival](https://amsterdamai.com/nl/events/amsterdam-ai-impact-festival-2026/) returned to the NEMO Science Museum in Amsterdam for its second edition. For us it was a first: the first time we took the AI Impact Registry out of the research setting and put it in front of the public.

## A conversation, not a presentation

The session, led by Evi-Anne van Dis of Amsterdam UMC, was part of the festival's programme on AI and mental wellbeing. We could have used the time to explain the Registry. Instead we spent most of it asking.

The question we put to the room was simple: if using an AI tool affected how you felt, how would you want to report it?

That opened up the questions we cannot answer on our own. What should count as a side effect worth reporting? Would you report something that happened to a friend, a partner or a child, or only to yourself? What would make you trust a tool like this with something personal? How much would you be willing to write, and how much would you rather pick from a list? And what would you want to get back for your report?

People came at it from very different places: some use AI tools every day, some barely at all, and some came because of what they had seen happen to someone close to them. That range is the point. The people who will notice these effects are not a special group, so the people shaping how they are reported should not be either.

## A first look inside

Towards the end we showed, for the first time, the reporting tool as it stands today: what someone sees when they start a report, the kinds of questions it asks, and how it keeps a report anonymous. It is an early version, and we showed it as one. The point was to put something concrete in front of people early enough that what they told us can still change it.

## What we take from it

What people said in the room goes straight into the next round of work on the tool: the wording of questions, the order they come in, what is optional and what is not, and how we explain what happens to a report once it is sent.

This was one afternoon and one room. It is a small beginning, and we intend it to be the first of many. A tool for reporting what AI does to people should be shaped by those people, not only by researchers, and we will keep coming back to the public as it develops.

If you have noticed something in yourself, or in someone close to you, that you suspect is linked to an AI tool, the Registry is being built for exactly that. The reporting tool is launching soon, [here](/report). And if you want to help shape it before then, we would like to hear from you.
