If you've used a chatbot to summarise a paper, draft an email, or make sense of a
pile of data, you've been part of the biggest experiment in research culture in
decades. Generative AI is everywhere in science - and for most of its short life,
the rules for using it have been a patchwork: one guideline from your university,
another from your funder, another from the journal you're submitting to.

That's starting to change.

## Scientists asked for rules that can keep up

In late 2023, a group of researchers made the case in [Nature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37857895/)
that scientists themselves must oversee how generative AI is used in research -
and that the guidance should be *living*: written to be updated as the technology
changes, instead of frozen at the moment it was published. The argument was
simple: AI tools bring real benefits to science - speed, accessibility, new ways
to work with knowledge - but those benefits depend on humans staying responsible
for the output.

The same team had already called for [five priorities for research with ChatGPT](https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-023-00288-7)
earlier that year. The living-guidelines idea was the follow-through: not a
one-time code, but a process.

## Europe made it official

That approach is no longer just a proposal in a journal. It's now [official
European guidance](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/document/download/2b6cf7e5-36ac-41cb-aab5-0d32050143dc_en?filename=ec_rtd_ai-guidelines.pdf):
the *Living guidelines on the responsible use of generative AI in research*,
published by the European Commission's research arm together with the ERA Forum -
now in its **third edition (May 2026)**, updated regularly by design.

The guidelines speak to researchers, research organisations, and research
funders. The core recommendations are strikingly plain:

- **You stay responsible.** AI systems are neither authors nor co-authors;
  researchers remain accountable for the integrity of what they produce, even
  when a tool helped.
- **Be transparent.** If generative AI substantially shaped the work, say so -
  in the methods, per your discipline's norms.
- **Mind the limits.** Bias, sycophantic answers, invented citations and
  black-box reasoning are named explicitly as things to check for, not to trust
  through.
- **Protect what's sensitive.** Don't upload unpublished work or personal data
  into tools that might train on it.

None of this is about banning the tools. It's about keeping the human in charge -
which is exactly the point the Nature comment made, now with the weight of
official policy behind it.

## Same experts, same spirit

Why does this matter here? Because this site comes from the same experts who
pushed for living guidelines - and the same conviction that careful, human
oversight of AI is a public-interest question, not just a lab-hygiene one.
[Reporting how an AI tool made you feel](/report) is part of that same spirit:
a way for observation by many people to feed back into oversight.

The rules for AI in research are official now. The reason they're "living" is
that the technology isn't done changing - and neither are we.
