Most of us don't notice the moment a tool starts to cost us something. The shift
is gradual: a little more time than we meant to spend, a slightly worse mood
afterwards, a thought we can't quite shake. This post is a short guide to
catching that earlier - and a reminder that noticing is enough to report it.

## Why this is hard to see

Conversational AI tools and social platforms are designed to hold attention.
When something is engineered to keep you engaged, the signal that it's *not*
serving you is easy to miss - it feels like your own choice, right up until it
doesn't.

You don't need to prove the tool caused how you feel. A suspected link is exactly
what this kind of reporting is for.

## Early signs worth noticing

- **Afterwards, not during.** You feel flat, anxious, or worse about yourself
  once you put the phone down - even if using it felt fine.
- **The clock disagrees with you.** You sit down for five minutes and look up an
  hour later, more often than you'd like.
- **It follows you.** A conversation or a post keeps replaying in your head when
  you're trying to do something else, or trying to sleep.
- **You feel smaller.** More self-critical, more compared-against, less sure of
  yourself than before you opened it.

None of these is a diagnosis. Any one of them, mild or strong, is worth paying
attention to.

## A small step you can take

If something above landed, you don't need to resolve every doubt first. Describe
what you were using and how you felt, in your own words, and let it be assessed.

> This is not a crisis or emergency service. If you or someone else is in
> immediate danger, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line first.

Reports like yours are read across many experiences at once - which is how a
pattern that no single person could see on their own starts to become visible.
